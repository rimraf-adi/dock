\section{Introduction}
De novo development of oncology therapeutics typically requires over a decade of preclinical and clinical trials, with capitalized expenditures regularly exceeding \$2.5 billion per approved entity \cite{pushpakom2019}. Despite advances in targeted therapies and immune checkpoint blockade, Phase II/III clinical trial attrition exceeds 90\%, primarily driven by dose-limiting toxicities, unanticipated off-target liabilities, and the emergence of Darwinian bypass resistance pathways \cite{corsello2020}. 

In contrast, computational and experimental drug repurposing accelerates therapeutic translation by evaluating regulatory-approved non-oncological agents whose human safety, tolerability, maximum tolerated dose (MTD), and pharmacokinetic profiles are thoroughly documented \cite{pushpakom2019}. Among candidate chemical classes, antiparasitic and anthelmintic drugs have repeatedly exhibited intriguing antineoplastic properties in phenotypic and preclinical models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Benzimidazoles} (Mebendazole, Fenbendazole, Albendazole) inhibit microtubule assembly, arrest cell cycle progression at the G2/M transition, induce apoptosis, and reactivate wild-type p53 signaling \cite{bai2011, guerini2019}.
    \item \textbf{Ivermectin} impairs nuclear import of oncogenic cargo via importin $\alpha/\beta$ blockade, modulates PAK1 signaling, and triggers immunogenic cancer cell death \cite{wagstaff2012}.
    \item \textbf{Niclosamide} exerts potent inhibitory effects on the Wnt/$\beta$-catenin, STAT3, and mTOR oncogenic cascades in colorectal and ovarian malignancies \cite{ren2010}.
    \item \textbf{Praziquantel}, the cornerstone anthelmintic for schistosomiasis, represents an under-investigated scaffold in oncology despite demonstrated modulation of cellular ion permeability and cytoskeletal dynamics \cite{probst2021}.
\end{itemize}

Most prior computational docking investigations have interrogated individual repurposed drugs against single isolated proteins. Consequently, a systematic, multi-target comparative assessment performed across diverse structural nodes of oncogenesis under uniform, literature-grounded docking conditions has been absent. 

In this investigation, we execute a cross-target in silico screen of 13 repurposed clinical drugs against 8 validated oncological target proteins. We parameterize binding grids based on experimental co-crystallized reference ligands, calculate ligand efficiency (LE) metrics, analyze all-atom residue contact networks, and benchmark our results directly against standard-of-care clinical chemotherapies.

\section{Computational Methods}

\subsection{Target Selection and Structural Curation}
Eight high-resolution crystallographic structures representing distinct hallmarks of cancer were retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{$\beta$-Tubulin (TUBB):} PDB \href{https://www.rcsb.org/structure/4O2B}{4O2B} (2.30\,\AA), co-crystallized with colchicine at the $\alpha/\beta$-tubulin dimer interface \cite{prota2014}.
    \item \textbf{Importin-$\alpha$ (KPNA2):} PDB \href{https://www.rcsb.org/structure/4WV6}{4WV6} (1.75\,\AA), complexed with an NLS peptide cargo \cite{pumroy2015}.
    \item \textbf{p21-Activated Kinase 1 (PAK1):} PDB \href{https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2HY8}{2HY8} (2.00\,\AA), ATP catalytic cleft co-crystallized with staurosporine analog ST2001.
    \item \textbf{Glucose Transporter 1 (GLUT1 / SLC2A1):} PDB \href{https://www.rcsb.org/structure/5EQI}{5EQI} (3.00\,\AA), inward-facing conformation complexed with cytochalasin B \cite{deng2014}.
    \item \textbf{STAT3 SH2 Domain:} PDB \href{https://www.rcsb.org/structure/6NJS}{6NJS} (2.70\,\AA), dimerization pocket complexed with inhibitor SI-109 \cite{bai2019}.
    \item \textbf{MDM2 Ubiquitin Ligase:} PDB \href{https://www.rcsb.org/structure/4HG7}{4HG7} (1.60\,\AA), p53 transactivation pocket complexed with piperidinone antagonist AMG 232 \cite{sun2014}.
    \item \textbf{Frizzled-4 CRD (FZD4):} PDB \href{https://www.rcsb.org/structure/6TFB}{6TFB} (1.68\,\AA), lipid-binding groove complexed with carbamazepine.
    \item \textbf{Thioredoxin Reductase 1 (TXNRD1):} PDB \href{https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2ZZB}{2ZZB} (3.20\,\AA), homodimeric active site complexed with NADPH.
\end{enumerate}

Crystallographic waters, cryoprotectants, and non-protein heteroatoms were stripped. Polar hydrogens were added, non-polar hydrogens merged, and Kollman/Gasteiger charges assigned using Biopython and OpenBabel. Reference co-crystallized inhibitors were extracted to establish pocket centroid boundaries.

\subsection{Ligand Preparation and Parameterization}
Three-dimensional SDF structures for the 13 candidate repurposed drugs were retrieved from PubChem: Fenbendazole (3334), Ivermectin (6321424), Mebendazole (4030), Albendazole (2082), Niclosamide (4477), Nitazoxanide (41684), Praziquantel (4891), Itraconazole (55283), Ketoconazole (456201), Metformin (4091), Disulfiram (3117), Chloroquine (2719), and Auranofin (6333901). Structures were minimized using the MMFF94 force field. Flexible torsions and partial atomic charges were assigned with Meeko. For the macrocycle Ivermectin (MW 875.1\,Da), rigid macrocyclic constraints were implemented to maintain conformational sampling bounds.

\subsection{Docking Protocol and Throttling}
Docking was conducted using native arm64 AutoDock Vina v1.2.7 \cite{trott2010}. Grid boxes were centered on the experimental co-crystal inhibitor centroids with a minimum 10\,\AA{} boundary buffer ($24 \times 24 \times 24$\,\AA{} up to $32 \times 32 \times 32$\,\AA{}). Dynamic exhaustiveness was applied: $\text{exhaustiveness} = 16$ for drug-like entities and $\text{exhaustiveness} = 8$ for extended macrocycles. Hardware execution was capped at 2 CPU cores at priority \texttt{nice -n 15} with inter-run cooldowns to prevent workstation thermal degradation.

\subsection{Efficiency Metrics and Contact Mapping}
Physicochemical descriptors were calculated with RDKit:
\begin{align}
    \text{Ligand Efficiency (LE)} &= \frac{-\Delta G}{N_{\text{heavy}}}\quad (\text{kcal}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{HA}^{-1}) \\
    \text{Lipophilic Efficiency (LipE)} &= pK_d - \text{cLogP} \approx \frac{-\Delta G}{1.363} - \text{cLogP}
\end{align}
Residue interactions were extracted from the top-scoring pose (MODEL 1) using Euclidean distance thresholds: putative hydrogen bonds ($D_{N,O,S \dots N,O,S} \le 3.5$\,\AA) and hydrophobic contacts ($D_{C \dots C} \le 4.0$\,\AA).

\section{Results}

\subsection{Global Screen Landscape}
A total of 96 organic compound-target complexes were evaluated. Calculated binding free energies ranged from $-12.17$\,kcal/mol (Ivermectin on GLUT1) to $+1.22$\,kcal/mol (Ivermectin on $\beta$-tubulin). Across all pairs, 42 (43.8\%) bound with strong affinity ($\le -7.0$\,kcal/mol), and 17 (17.7\%) achieved top-tier affinity ($\le -8.5$\,kcal/mol) (Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}).

\subsection{Praziquantel: Discovery of a High-Affinity Tubulin and GLUT1 Antagonist}
Praziquantel (PRA) emerged as the most surprising hit of the study:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Microtubule Binding:} PRA achieved an affinity of \textbf{$-10.08$\,kcal/mol} on $\beta$-tubulin, surpassing Mebendazole ($-9.56$\,kcal/mol), Fenbendazole ($-9.10$\,kcal/mol), and Albendazole ($-7.22$\,kcal/mol).
    \item \textbf{Transporter Blockade:} PRA docked into the GLUT1 translocation cavity with \textbf{$-9.63$\,kcal/mol}.
    \item \textbf{MDM2 Engagement:} PRA bound MDM2 with \textbf{$-8.82$\,kcal/mol}, the highest score recorded for MDM2.
    \item \textbf{High Ligand Efficiency:} PRA possesses a compact molecular weight (312.4\,Da, 23 heavy atoms) and modest lipophilicity ($\text{cLogP} = 2.53$), yielding an exceptional \textbf{$LE = 0.438$\,kcal/mol/HA} and \textbf{$\text{LipE} = 4.86$} on $\beta$-tubulin (Figure~\ref{fig:le}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Broad Polypharmacological Profile of Mebendazole}
Mebendazole (MEB) bound \textbf{all 8 out of 8 targets} with strong affinity ($\le -7.0$\,kcal/mol, mean $-8.09$\,kcal/mol) (Figure~\ref{fig:radar}): $\beta$-tubulin ($-9.56$), GLUT1 ($-8.60$), TXNRD1 ($-8.47$), MDM2 ($-8.24$), Frizzled-4 ($-8.03$), Importin-$\alpha$ ($-7.81$), PAK1 ($-7.01$), and STAT3 ($-7.01$). This multi-target breadth provides a structural foundation for mebendazole's clinical activity in recurrent glioblastoma and refractory cancers \cite{bai2011, guerini2019}.

\subsection{Recapitulation of Known Mechanisms}
The virtual screen validated known biochemical mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Niclosamide on STAT3:} Niclosamide bound the STAT3 SH2 dimerization domain ($-8.44$\,kcal/mol), forming hydrogen bonds with Arg609 (2.68\,\AA), Ser611 (2.70\,\AA), Ser613 (2.80\,\AA), and Ser636 (2.99\,\AA)---the identical pocket coordinating pTyr705 for dimerization \cite{bai2019}.
    \item \textbf{Ivermectin on Importin-$\alpha$:} Ivermectin occupied the NLS groove with $-9.47$\,kcal/mol, in concordance with its documented nuclear import blockade \cite{wagstaff2012}.
    \item \textbf{Physical Discrimination Negative Control:} In contrast, Ivermectin was sterically excluded from the tubulin colchicine pocket ($+1.22$\,kcal/mol), validating grid box specificity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Therapeutic Rationale for Polypharmacology}
Single-target kinase inhibitors frequently succumb to Darwinian clonal selection and secondary point mutations. Polypharmacology---the simultaneous disruption of multiple complementary oncogenic signaling circuits---represents a compelling strategy for preventing resistance:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Concurrent Mitotic and Metabolic Catastrophe:} Malignant cells depend on aerobic glycolysis (the Warburg effect) and rapid microtubule spindle turnover. Dual inhibition of tubulin ($\le -9.5$\,kcal/mol) and glucose influx through GLUT1 ($\le -8.6$\,kcal/mol) induces synthetic energetic catastrophe. Both mebendazole and praziquantel exhibit this dual-action profile.
    \item \textbf{p53 Reactivation via MDM2 Displacement:} Mebendazole's calculated binding to MDM2 ($-8.24$\,kcal/mol, Table~\ref{tab:chemo}) provides a mechanistic rationale for the previously unexplained p53 accumulation observed in benzimidazole-treated cancer cells \cite{guerini2019}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Praziquantel: An Actionable Lead for Neuro-Oncology}
While mebendazole is undergoing active clinical evaluation in high-grade gliomas, praziquantel has remained neglected in cancer drug development. In this screen, praziquantel achieved the highest tubulin affinity ($-10.08$\,kcal/mol) and highest Ligand Efficiency ($0.438$\,kcal/mol/HA) of any drug-like compound evaluated, outperforming colchicine and nocodazole. With a proven human safety profile spanning millions of treated patients and excellent CNS penetration, praziquantel represents an attractive candidate for repurposing in glioblastoma and taxane-refractory tumors.

\subsection{Methodological Considerations}
Limitations of this study include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rigid Receptor Approximation:} While co-crystallized conformations provide induced-fit active site geometries, receptor flexibility may modulate precise absolute binding energies.
    \item \textbf{Metallodrug Parameterization:} Auranofin contains a gold(I) coordination center. Standard empirical force fields cannot capture metal coordination geometry or covalent transfer to selenocysteine residues in TXNRD1, requiring hybrid QM/MM approaches.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Roadmap}
We recommend the following experimental validations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{All-Atom Molecular Dynamics (100\,ns):} Execute explicit-solvent MD (OpenMM/GROMACS) on PRA–BTUB, MEB–BTUB, and NIC–STA3 to calculate MM-PBSA binding free energies ($\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$) and monitor root-mean-square fluctuations (RMSF).
    \item \textbf{Microtubule Assembly Turbidimetry:} Quantify inhibition of porcine brain tubulin polymerization by praziquantel, mebendazole, and colchicine at 340\,nm.
    \item \textbf{Glioblastoma Viability Assays:} Assess $IC_{50}$ and synergism of praziquantel alone and combined with temozolomide in U87MG and U251 glioblastoma cell lines.
    \item \textbf{Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR):} Measure physical binding kinetics ($K_D$, $k_{\text{on}}$, $k_{\text{off}}$) for praziquantel against recombinant $\beta$-tubulin and GLUT1.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This systematic in silico investigation establishes the structural basis for the polypharmacological anticancer efficacy of repurposed therapeutics. We discover that \textbf{Praziquantel} is a high-affinity microtubule and GLUT1 antagonist possessing exceptional Ligand Efficiency and blood-brain barrier permeability, and confirm the multi-target potency of \textbf{Mebendazole} across eight validated oncological targets. These findings provide immediate, actionable hypotheses for preclinical drug repurposing pipelines.
